We are given an undirected weighted graph with nodes:

**Bay, Drew, Moss, Oak, Poe, Sage, Vale, Zed**

We are to find the **shortest path from Bay to Vale** in terms of total edge weight.

---

### Step 1: List all edges with weights

From the input:

- Bay — Zed: 5  
- Drew — Moss: 5  
- Drew — Oak: 3  
- Drew — Zed: 6  
- Moss — Poe: 8  
- Moss — Vale: 7  
- Moss — Zed: 4  
- Oak — Vale: 1  
- Oak — Zed: 7  
- Poe — Vale: 4  
- Poe — Zed: 2  
- Sage — Zed: 5  

Note: The graph is undirected, so each edge is bidirectional.

We are to find the shortest path from **Bay to Vale**.

---

### Step 2: Consider possible paths from Bay to Vale

Bay is only directly connected to **Zed** (weight 5).  
So all paths from Bay must go through Zed (or through other nodes connected to Zed).

So we can think of paths as:

**Bay → Zed → ... → Vale**

Or possibly through other intermediate nodes, but since Bay only connects to Zed, all paths must go through Zed.

So we can reframe the problem:  
Find the shortest path from **Zed to Vale**, then add the 5 from Bay → Zed.
