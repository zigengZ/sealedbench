We are given an undirected weighted graph with 9 nodes and several edges. We are to find the **shortest path from Cedar to Xen** and report the **total weight** of that path.

---

### Step 1: List all edges with weights

From the input:

- Bay – Kai: 2  
- Bay – Reed: 5  
- Bay – Xen: 6  
- Cedar – Nell: 5  
- Cedar – Reed: 3  
- Cedar – Uri: 7  
- Kai – Lark: 7  
- Lark – Tess: 2  
- Nell – Tess: 6  
- Tess – Xen: 2  

All edges are undirected.

---

### Step 2: Goal — Shortest path from **Cedar** to **Xen**

We will perform a shortest path algorithm (like Dijkstra’s) manually, starting from Cedar.

We’ll keep track of distances from Cedar to each node.

Initialize:

- Distance to Cedar: 0  
- All others: ∞

We’ll update distances as we explore neighbors.

---

### Step 3: Breadth-first/Dijkstra-style exploration

Start with Cedar (distance = 0)

**From Cedar:**
- To Nell: 5 → distance[Nell] = 5  
- To Reed: 3 → distance[Reed] = 3  
- To Uri: 7 → distance[Uri] = 7  

Current distances:
- Cedar: 0  
- Nell: 5  
- Reed: 3  
- Uri: 7  
- Others: ∞

Now, process the node with smallest distance: **Reed (3)**

**From Reed:**
- Connected to Bay (5), Cedar (3), and Cedar (already visited)

Reed → Bay: weight 5  
So, distance to Bay = 3 + 5 = 8  
(Previously Bay was ∞, now 8)

Now distances:
- Bay: 8  
- Others: as before

Next smallest: **Nell (5)**

**From Nell:**
- Connected to Cedar (5), Tess (6)

So, Nell → Tess: 5 + 6 = 11  
→ distance[Tess] = 11

Now distances:
- Tess: 11

Next: **Tess (11)**

**From Tess:**
- Connected to Nell (6), Lark (2), Xen (2)

Tess → Lark: 11 + 2 = 13  
Tess → Xen: 11 + 2 = 13  
→ distance[Xen] = 13

Now we have:
- Xen: 13

Now check if any other path to Xen can be shorter.

We also have:
- Bay → Xen: 6  
- Bay is at distance 8 → Bay → Xen = 8 + 6 = 14 → worse than 13

Any other path?

What about via Lark?

We have:
- Lark → Tess (2), Kai (7)

We have Tess at 11 → Lark would be 11 + 2 = 13 (from Tess), but we can also go from Kai to Lark.

But we haven’t reached Kai yet.

Can we reach Kai?

From Bay → Kai: 2  
Bay is at 8 → Bay → Kai = 8 + 2 = 10 → Kai = 10

Then Kai → Lark: 7 → Lark = 10 + 7 = 17 → then Lark → Tess = 17 + 2
