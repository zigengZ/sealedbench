We are given an undirected weighted graph with 10 nodes and several edges. We are to find the **shortest path weight** between **Xen** and **York**.

---

### Step 1: List all nodes
Nodes:  
Amber, Bay, Blair, Eden, Glen, Kai, Oak, Tess, Xen, York

---

### Step 2: List all edges with weights

From the input:

- Amber – Glen: 9  
- Amber – Oak: 2  
- Amber – Tess: 5  
- Bay – Tess: 4  
- Blair – Eden: 2  
- Eden – Kai: 2  
- Eden – Xen: 8  
- Glen – Oak: 3  
- Glen – Tess: 9  
- Kai – Oak: 8  
- Kai – York: 9  

Note: All edges are undirected, so each edge is bidirectional.

---

### Step 3: Goal — Shortest path from **Xen** to **York**

We need to find the shortest path (in terms of total edge weight) from **Xen** to **York**.

We can use **Breadth-First Search (BFS)** or **Dijkstra’s algorithm** since all weights are positive.

We’ll use **Dijkstra’s algorithm** to compute shortest paths from Xen to all other nodes.

---

### Step 4: Build adjacency list

We’ll build the graph from the edges:

- Amber: Glen(9), Oak(2), Tess(5)  
- Bay: Tess(4)  
- Blair: Eden(2)  
- Eden: Blair(2), Kai(2), Xen(8)  
- Glen: Amber(9), Oak(3), Tess(9)  
- Kai: Eden(2), Oak(8), York(9)  
- Oak: Amber(2), Glen(3), Kai(8)  
- Tess: Amber(5), Bay(4), Glen(9)  
- Xen: Eden(8)  
- York: Kai(9)

---

### Step 5: Run Dijkstra from Xen

We start at **Xen**, and want to reach **York**.

Initialize distances:
- dist[Xen] = 0
- All others = ∞

Queue: (Xen, 0)

---

**Step 1**: Process Xen (dist = 0)  
From Xen: only edge to Eden (weight 8)  
→ Update dist[Eden] = 8

Queue: (Eden, 8)

---

**Step 2**: Process Eden (dist = 8)  
From Eden:  
- to Blair (2): 8 + 2 = 10 → update dist[Blair] = 10  
- to Kai (2): 8 + 2 = 10 → update dist[Kai] = 10  
- to Xen (8): already visited  

Queue: (Blair, 10), (Kai, 10)

---

**Step 3**: Process Blair (dist = 10)  
Blair only connects to Eden (already processed) → no new updates

Queue: (Kai, 10)

---

**Step 4**: Process Kai (dist = 10)  
From Kai:  
- to Eden (2): already visited  
- to Oak (8): 10 + 8 = 18 → update dist[Oak] = 18  
- to York (9): 10 + 9 = 19 → update dist[York] = 19  

Now we have:
- dist[York] = 19
